\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:literature}

\subsection{Trip planning and information in the field}
Brown and Chalmers used field observations and diaries to study how tourists combine information sources while moving through a city \cite{brown}. Their findings motivate packaging the day's plan with its tickets. In GUIDE, Cheverst \emph{et al.} adapted a city guide to the visitor and their surroundings \cite{guide}. TripyTravy presents the agent's schedule and chosen locations. A location can open in a map app while the itinerary remains available locally.

Gavalas \emph{et al.} classify trip-planning problems by attractions, available time, and traveler constraints \cite{ttdp}. The City Trip Planner selects and orders attractions under time and opening-hour constraints \cite{city}. De Choudhury \emph{et al.} derive candidate routes from geotagged photos \cite{breadcrumbs}, and Lim \emph{et al.} estimate interests and visit durations from travel sequences to recommend tours \cite{perstour}. Adamo \emph{et al.} model driving tours with walking legs from parking locations \cite{multimodal}. TripyTravy starts from dates, durations, meeting points, and transport details entered by the agent. It passes locations to the map app in event order and leaves route calculation to that app.

\subsection{Offline records and update semantics}
Local-first software connects device-local work to data ownership and offline access \cite{localfirst}. Coda demonstrates disconnected work through caching and reintegration when the server becomes reachable \cite{coda}. TripyTravy receives local data from an explicitly transferred snapshot, so its reader has no server reintegration step.

Bayou uses dependency checks and application-specific merge procedures for independent writes \cite{bayou}. Dynamo combines object versioning with reconciliation for divergent replicas \cite{dynamo}. Conflict-free replicated data types specify how replica updates converge \cite{crdt}, and Unison formalizes file synchronization from a common baseline and two replica states \cite{unison}. TripyTravy has one publisher per editable itinerary, so the Viewer can order exports by a revision counter without merging schedules. Neither device time nor event time determines that order, a distinction that follows Lamport's separation of event ordering from physical time \cite{lamport}. A close application precedent is iCalendar's \texttt{UID} and \texttt{SEQUENCE}, which let a client recognize a rescheduled event \cite{itip}.

\subsection{Storage and security}
Venti uses fingerprints to store duplicate blocks once in archival storage \cite{venti}. TripyTravy hashes whole attachments. The Low-Bandwidth File System reuses matching chunks across files and versions \cite{lbfs}. TripyTravy sends the entire snapshot on every revision, so its export savings come from duplicate content within that package. Research on crash consistency explains why a staged file replacement alone cannot demonstrate recovery across several persisted records \cite{crash}.

Saltzer and Schroeder distinguish open design and least privilege from simply recognizing a format \cite{saltzer}. Felt \emph{et al.} analyze permissions in Android applications \cite{permissions}. TripyTravy grants a document app access to a selected file through an Android URI, while the transfer package has its own authentication check. Samuel \emph{et al.} examine signing roles and key compromise in update systems \cite{tuf}, and Mercury measures the bandwidth cost of authenticated rollback protection \cite{mercury}. TripyTravy's Viewer keeps the newer revision once it saves it. The protected format authenticates each package under a shared passphrase. A separate mechanism would be needed to identify its publisher. The application codec uses Argon2 to raise the cost of guessing a password offline \cite{argon}. Section~\ref{sec:container} describes the codec.

\subsection{Travel software and scope of comparison}
KDE Itinerary supports whole-trip export and can suggest a matching trip when a ticket arrives \cite{kde}. Travefy supports agent authoring and traveler delivery \cite{travefybuilder}. Its hosted link reflects edits when the traveler opens or refreshes it \cite{travefyupdates}, and its mobile app supports offline download \cite{travefyoffline}. Section~\ref{sec:comparison} uses the vendor documentation to compare when travelers receive an itinerary and how its revisions reach their devices.
