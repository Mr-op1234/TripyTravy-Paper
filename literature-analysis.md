# Literature analysis

This targeted review covers 21 conference or journal papers and one research report. It uses the publications, author copies or publisher abstracts linked below. The scope is itinerary authoring and travel use, disconnected operation, storage, and protection of file-based updates. Standards and product manuals are separate supporting references in the manuscript.

## Travel workflows

- **Barry Brown et al. (2003).** Ethnographic observations and diaries describe tourists combining information sources while moving through a city. Motivates retaining schedule and supporting material together. TripyTravy did not replicate the field study. [Tourism and Mobile Technology](https://www.dourish.com/classes/readings/BrownChalmers-Tourism-ECSCW.pdf)
- **Keith Cheverst et al. (2000).** The GUIDE deployment uses visitor and environmental context to present city information and support tours. A reference for the in-trip role. TripyTravy presents locations and schedules supplied by the author. [Developing a Context-Aware Electronic Tourist Guide: Some Issues and Experiences](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/38650/)
- **Damianos Gavalas et al. (2014).** Classifies tourist route-planning models by point attributes and trip constraints. Separates algorithmic selection and routing from editing and delivering an authored trip. [A Survey on Algorithmic Approaches for Solving Tourist Trip Design Problems](https://www.syros.aegean.gr/users/dgavalas/en/iframe_files/papers/2014/survey_TTDP.pdf)
- **Pieter Vansteenwegen et al. (2011).** The planner selects and orders attractions under available time and opening-hour constraints. TripyTravy stores event dates and times as author inputs. It supplies no matching optimization benchmark. [The City Trip Planner: An Expert System for Tourists](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/1086438)
- **Munmun De Choudhury et al. (2010).** Derives candidate sightseeing sequences from geotagged, time-stamped photo traces. The Creator uses manual event and location entry and does not learn from traveler traces. [Automatic Construction of Travel Itineraries Using Social Breadcrumbs](https://cris.openu.ac.il/en/publications/automatic-construction-of-travel-itineraries-using-social-breadcr-2/)
- **Kwan Hui Lim et al. (2015).** Models interests and visit duration from travel sequences derived from photos and evaluates on four cities. TripyTravy's durations are author-entered. The functional evaluation addresses record fidelity and update behavior. [Personalized Tour Recommendation Based on User Interests and Points of Interest Visit Durations](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2015/0253.pdf)
- **Tommaso Adamo et al. (2024).** Combines road travel with pedestrian subtours starting at parking locations. The Viewer forwards ordered locations to Google Maps. It does not implement this network optimization. [A Multi-Modal Tourist Trip Planner Integrating Road and Pedestrian Networks](https://airus.unisalento.it/retrieve/64e94470-6c5e-4c52-ae1b-b1fd82ddb68b/1-s2.0-S0957417423019590-main.pdf)

## Disconnected operation and revision ordering

- **Kleppmann et al. (2019).** The paper connects offline availability and ownership and examines approaches including file exchange and replicated state. TripyTravy uses portable snapshots and a single writer for the itinerary. [Local-First Software](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2019/10/23/local-first-at-onward.html)
- **James J. Kistler et al. (1992).** Caches data for disconnected work and reintegrates updates after reconnection. An imported trip is an independently readable artifact. TripyTravy has no server reintegration phase. [Disconnected Operation in the Coda File System](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~satya/docdir/kistler-tocs-coda-1992.pdf)
- **Douglas B. Terry et al. (1995).** Uses dependency checks and merge procedures to resolve conflicts across writable replicas. The Viewer consumes full snapshots from one editor. A revision comparison is sufficient for that workflow. [Managing Update Conflicts in Bayou, a Weakly Connected Replicated Storage System](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall22/cos418/papers/bayou.pdf)
- **Giuseppe DeCandia et al. (2007).** Combines object versioning with application-assisted resolution across replicas. TripyTravy tracks one export counter per trip. The counter does not encode divergent authorship. [Dynamo: Amazon's Highly Available Key-Value Store](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/papers/amazon-dynamo-sosp2007.pdf)
- **Marc Shapiro et al. (2011).** Describes convergence conditions for state- and operation-based replicated data types. The paper bounds TripyTravy's claim to ordered snapshots and local traveler state. [Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-24550-3_29)
- **Leslie Lamport (1978).** Distinguishes causal order from physical time and introduces logical clocks. The export counter orders one publisher's snapshots independently of clock time or travel time zones. [Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System](https://lamport.org/pubs/time-clocks.pdf)
- **Benjamin C. Pierce et al. (2004).** Models file synchronization from a common baseline and changes on two replicas. TripyTravy applies a one-way replacement rule and keeps checklist state outside the publisher's snapshot. [What's in Unison? A Formal Specification and Reference Implementation of a File Synchronizer](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~bcpierce/papers/unisonspec.pdf)

## Storage and persistence

- **Athicha Muthitacharoen et al. (2001).** Uses content-dependent chunking and caches to avoid retransmitting unchanged regions. TripyTravy deduplicates whole blobs within an export and still sends a complete package. [A Low-Bandwidth Network File System](https://www.sosp.org/2001/papers/mazieres.pdf)
- **Sean Quinlan et al. (2002).** Uses block fingerprints for content addressing and duplicate suppression in archival storage. TripyTravy addresses complete document and image blobs by SHA-256, independent of filenames. [Venti: A New Approach to Archival Storage](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast02/quinlan/quinlan_html/index.html)
- **Thanumalayan Sankaranarayana Pillai et al. (2014).** Shows that persistence properties vary and that implicit assumptions can break application recovery. One staged rename cannot establish crash consistency across trip, asset, and checklist records. [All File Systems Are Not Created Equal: On the Complexity of Crafting Crash-Consistent Applications](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi14/technical-sessions/presentation/pillai)

## Permissions and update security

- **Jerome H. Saltzer et al. (1975).** Explains open design, least privilege, and access control as distinct protection principles. A proprietary extension gives format recognition. Protection requires a defined trust model and keys. [The Protection of Information in Computer Systems](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~cs9242/current/papers/Saltzer_Schroeder_75.pdf)
- **Adrienne Porter Felt et al. (2011).** Maps Android API calls to permissions and studies excess privileges. Motivates discussing the document handler's URI grant separately from the container threat model. [Android Permissions Demystified](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/134681/files/EECS-2011-48.pdf)
- **Justin Samuel et al. (2010).** Describes signing roles and designs for limiting damage from compromised update keys. Identifies missing publisher authentication and key-management decisions in the current update path. [Survivable Key Compromise in Software Update Systems](https://theupdateframework.github.io/papers/survivable-key-compromise-ccs2010.pdf)
- **Trishank Karthik Kuppusamy et al. (2017).** Studies authenticated rollback prevention and its metadata cost in package repositories. The importer's stale-file check is a functional check. Its revision field has no cryptographic provenance. [Mercury: Bandwidth-Effective Prevention of Rollback Attacks Against Community Repositories](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc17/technical-sessions/presentation/kuppusamy)
- **Alex Biryukov et al. (2016).** Develops a memory-hard password hashing family. The linked author preprint supplies the design context. Explains the goal of the Rust prototype's PIN derivation without attributing it to either app. [Argon2: New Generation of Memory-Hard Functions for Password Hashing and Other Applications](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/430)
